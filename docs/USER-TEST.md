# Chogchemy holder feedback test

This is a blank plan for collecting real feedback. No independent participant results or retention measurements have been recorded in this document.

## A five-minute hands-on test

Recruit three to five Chog holders outside the build team. Use an anonymous participant ID such as H01 to distinguish people; multiple wallets belonging to the same person do not count as separate users. Record the date, device/browser and whether this is their first visit. Ask permission before quoting their feedback publicly.

The test takes roughly five minutes of active use. Image generation can take additional time; record that wait separately. If the drawing allowance is exhausted, mark the creation task blocked rather than treating the participant as a successful creator.

1. **First impression, 30 seconds.** Open the homepage. Ask: “What do you think this lets you do?” Avoid explaining the answer first.
2. **Find a joke, one minute.** Ask them to choose a template and explain which character their Chog will replace. Try a caption suggestion, then write or edit one caption. Record any confusion about labels or where text will appear.
3. **Create, one minute plus generation wait.** Connect their own holder wallet, select a Chog and create one meme. Joining the weekly prompt is their choice. Record success, error text, confusing steps, time until the request starts and time until the image is saved. No private keys are needed.
4. **Inspect and share, one minute.** Ask whether the Chog is recognizable and whether the joke works. Download it or open its public link. Let them choose whether to send it to a real chat; do not count clicking Share as proof that a message was sent.
5. **Keep the character, one minute.** Open the Chog passport and ask who created the images and what would happen if the NFT changed owners. Reopen the saved meme. As its original creator, change a caption and export without another drawing request.
6. **Closing question, 30 seconds.** Ask: “What would make you use this again?” Record their own words and the one change they most want.

Do not give participants a script that tells them the desired opinion. An honest failure or confusing step is useful evidence.

## Measure actual return use

Return use requires a second visit or action on a later day. Saying “I would come back” is not a return. With a participant's agreement, check once after 24–72 hours, or let them voluntarily send a second saved creation link. Do not imply automatic analytics exist: this build currently supports inspecting actual saved creation records and collecting feedback manually.

For each participant, record the follow-up date, whether they returned, what they did, and a saved result URL or a directly observed activity. Label self-reported returns separately from verified actions. A second drawing must use the normal allowance. Free caption editing or revisiting a passport can be recorded as a different return action, not as a newly generated meme.

Report counts with a clear denominator: people invited, independent people tested, people who completed a creation, people eligible for a full follow-up window, and people who actually returned. Keep builder testing separate. Report raw counts before percentages when the sample is small. If nobody returns, report zero and use the feedback to improve the next test.

## Artist review of real outputs

Ask @theejhay01 to review real saved images across several Chog designs and orientations. For each result, record the template, token ID, public URL and specific visible problems: missing traits, wrong character, unreadable joke, changed panel layout or inconsistent face. Compare the original NFT and source template with the saved output. Fixture images and manually drawn artist examples do not establish AI output quality.

Keep unfavorable results in the internal review record. Public examples can be selected for clarity, but describe the selection honestly and do not invent usage, ratings, participants or testimonials.
